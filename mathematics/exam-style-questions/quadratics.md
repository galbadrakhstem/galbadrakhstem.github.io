These are original practice questions, not copied past-paper questions. [M1] denotes method, [A1] accuracy and [B1] an independent result. These marks are illustrative, not an official CAIE scheme. Attempt each question before reading the solution.

## Question 1

<section class="sa-question"><p><span class="problem-no">1.</span> Solve $2x^2+3x-2=0$ by factorising. <span class="pts">[4]</span></p></section>

<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Full solution and illustrative marks</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>Split: $2x^2+4x-x-2=0$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Group: $2x(x+2)-(x+2)=0$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Factorise: $(2x-1)(x+2)=0$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Set factors to zero: $2x-1=0$ or $x+2=0$.</p></li>
<li><p>From the first: $2x=1$.</p></li>
<li><p>Divide: $x=\tfrac12$.</p></li>
<li><p>From the second: $x=-2$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
</ol></section>
<p class="key-takeaway"><strong>Final answer.</strong> $x=\frac12$ or $x=-2$ (exact).</p>
<aside class="examiner"><p><strong>Where marks are lost and how to show working</strong></p><ul>
<li>Show the split middle term to earn method credit.</li>
<li>Do not divide by $x+2$ and lose a root.</li>
<li>State both solutions explicitly.</li>
</ul></aside>

## Question 2

<section class="sa-question"><p><span class="problem-no">2.</span> For $f(x)=3x^2-12x+10$, (a) complete the square; (b) state its minimum; (c) solve $f(x)=1$. <span class="pts">[6]</span></p></section>

<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Full solution and illustrative marks</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>Factor: $f(x)=3(x^2-4x)+10$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Half $-4$: $-2$.</p></li>
<li><p>Square: $(-2)^2=4$.</p></li>
<li><p>Identity: $x^2-4x=(x-2)^2-4$.</p></li>
<li><p>Substitute: $f(x)=3((x-2)^2-4)+10$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Distribute: $f(x)=3(x-2)^2-12+10$.</p></li>
<li><p>Combine: $f(x)=3(x-2)^2-2$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Since $(x-2)^2\ge0$, the lowest value is $-2$ at $x=2$. <span class="mark">[B1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Set $f(x)=1$: $3(x-2)^2-2=1$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Add $2$: $3(x-2)^2=3$.</p></li>
<li><p>Divide: $(x-2)^2=1$.</p></li>
<li><p>Both branches: $x-2=\pm1$.</p></li>
<li><p>Add $2$: $x=2\pm1$.</p></li>
<li><p>Plus branch: $x=3$.</p></li>
<li><p>Minus branch: $x=1$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
</ol></section>
<p class="key-takeaway"><strong>Final answer.</strong> $f(x)=3(x-2)^2-2$; minimum $-2$ at $x=2$; $x=1$ or $3$ (exact).</p>
<aside class="examiner"><p><strong>Where marks are lost and how to show working</strong></p><ul>
<li>Factor out $3$ before completing the square.</li>
<li>Keep the compensating $-4$ inside the bracket.</li>
<li>Retain both square-root branches.</li>
</ul></aside>

## Question 3

<section class="sa-question"><p><span class="problem-no">3.</span> Solve $2x^2-3x-2&gt;0$ for real $x$. <span class="pts">[5]</span></p></section>

<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Full solution and illustrative marks</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>Factorise: $(2x+1)(x-2)&gt;0$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>First boundary: $2x+1=0$.</p></li>
<li><p>Subtract $1$: $2x=-1$.</p></li>
<li><p>Divide: $x=-\tfrac12$.</p></li>
<li><p>Second boundary: $x-2=0$.</p></li>
<li><p>Add: $x=2$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>For $x&lt;-\tfrac12$, both factors are negative: product positive. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>For $-\tfrac12&lt;x&lt;2$, factors have opposite signs: product negative.</p></li>
<li><p>For $x&gt;2$, both factors are positive: product positive.</p></li>
<li><p>Select $x&lt;-\tfrac12$ or $x&gt;2$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Exclude endpoints for a strict inequality. <span class="mark">[B1]</span></p></li>
</ol></section>
<p class="key-takeaway"><strong>Final answer.</strong> $x&lt;-\frac12$ or $x&gt;2$ (exact).</p>
<aside class="examiner"><p><strong>Where marks are lost and how to show working</strong></p><ul>
<li>Do not include boundary roots with a strict inequality.</li>
<li>Show the sign of the product on all three intervals.</li>
<li>Check the negative sign of the first boundary.</li>
</ul></aside>

## Question 4

<section class="sa-question"><p><span class="problem-no">4.</span> A line $y=2x+k$ meets $y=x^2-2x+4$. Find $k$ for exactly one real intersection and the point. <span class="pts">[6]</span></p></section>

<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Full solution and illustrative marks</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>Equate: $2x+k=x^2-2x+4$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Subtract $2x$: $k=x^2-4x+4$.</p></li>
<li><p>Subtract $k$: $0=x^2-4x+4-k$.</p></li>
<li><p>Identify $a=1$, $b=-4$, $c=4-k$.</p></li>
<li><p>Substitute: $\Delta=(-4)^2-4(1)(4-k)$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Square: $\Delta=16-4(1)(4-k)$.</p></li>
<li><p>Multiply: $\Delta=16-4(4-k)$.</p></li>
<li><p>Distribute: $\Delta=16-16+4k$.</p></li>
<li><p>Combine: $\Delta=4k$.</p></li>
<li><p>Tangency condition: $4k=0$.</p></li>
<li><p>Divide: $k=0$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Substitute: $x^2-4x+4=0$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Factorise: $(x-2)^2=0$.</p></li>
<li><p>Zero square: $x-2=0$.</p></li>
<li><p>Add: $x=2$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Substitute in line: $y=2(2)+0$.</p></li>
<li><p>Multiply: $y=4+0$.</p></li>
<li><p>Add: $y=4$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
</ol></section>
<p class="key-takeaway"><strong>Final answer.</strong> $k=0$; intersection $(2,4)$ (exact).</p>
<aside class="examiner"><p><strong>Where marks are lost and how to show working</strong></p><ul>
<li>Use a zero discriminant after eliminating $y$.</li>
<li>Retain $-k$ in the constant term.</li>
<li>Give both coordinates, not only $k$.</li>
</ul></aside>

## Question 5

<section class="sa-question"><p><span class="problem-no">5.</span> Solve $x+\sqrt x-20=0$ over the reals; explain any rejected root. <span class="pts">[5]</span></p></section>

<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Full solution and illustrative marks</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>Real domain: $x\ge0$; define $t=\sqrt x\ge0$. <span class="mark">[B1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Square definition: $x=t^2$.</p></li>
<li><p>Substitute $x$: $t^2+\sqrt x-20=0$.</p></li>
<li><p>Substitute root: $t^2+t-20=0$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Factorise: $(t+5)(t-4)=0$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Zero product: $t+5=0$ or $t-4=0$.</p></li>
<li><p>First root: $t=-5$.</p></li>
<li><p>Second root: $t=4$.</p></li>
<li><p>Reject $-5$ because $t\ge0$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Recover $x=4^2$.</p></li>
<li><p>Square: $x=16$.</p></li>
<li><p>Original left side: $16+\sqrt{16}-20$.</p></li>
<li><p>Root: $16+4-20$.</p></li>
<li><p>Add: $20-20$.</p></li>
<li><p>Subtract: $0$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
</ol></section>
<p class="key-takeaway"><strong>Final answer.</strong> $x=16$ (exact and verified).</p>
<aside class="examiner"><p><strong>Where marks are lost and how to show working</strong></p><ul>
<li>State the domain restriction before factorising.</li>
<li>Discard the negative value of the principal square root.</li>
<li>Verify the answer in the original equation.</li>
</ul></aside>

## Question 6

<section class="sa-question"><p><span class="problem-no">6.</span> For real $q$, $x^2-2qx+q^2-25=0$ has roots $r&lt;s$. Show $s-r=10$ and find $rs$. <span class="pts">[6]</span></p></section>

<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Full solution and illustrative marks</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>Identity: $(x-q)^2=x^2-2qx+q^2$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Substitute: $(x-q)^2-25=0$.</p></li>
<li><p>Add $25$: $(x-q)^2=25$.</p></li>
<li><p>Both roots: $x-q=\pm\sqrt{25}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Simplify: $x-q=\pm5$.</p></li>
<li><p>Add $q$: $x=q\pm5$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Smaller root: $r=q-5$.</p></li>
<li><p>Larger root: $s=q+5$.</p></li>
<li><p>Difference: $s-r=(q+5)-(q-5)$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Expand: $s-r=q+5-q+5$.</p></li>
<li><p>Cancel: $s-r=5+5$.</p></li>
<li><p>Add: $s-r=10$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Product: $rs=(q-5)(q+5)$. <span class="mark">[M1]</span></p></li>
<li><p>Expand: $rs=q^2+5q-5q-25$.</p></li>
<li><p>Cancel: $rs=q^2-25$. <span class="mark">[A1]</span></p></li>
</ol></section>
<p class="key-takeaway"><strong>Final answer.</strong> $r=q-5$, $s=q+5$; $s-r=10$, $rs=q^2-25$ (exact).</p>
<aside class="examiner"><p><strong>Where marks are lost and how to show working</strong></p><ul>
<li>Show both square-root branches.</li>
<li>Subtract the smaller root from the larger.</li>
<li>Show the product expansion for accuracy credit.</li>
</ul></aside>
