## What you need to be able to do

- Count arrangements of objects in a line, including arrangements with repeated items and with restrictions such as objects that must be together or apart.
- Use the notation $n!$ and $\binom{n}{r}$ and evaluate expressions with it.
- Count selections of objects where order does not matter, and say why a problem is a permutation rather than a combination.
- Solve problems involving a mixture of arrangements and selections, for example forming a committee from groups of different sizes.
