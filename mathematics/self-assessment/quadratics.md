# Fluency questions

Attempt each original question before reading its solution. These are not official examination questions; answers stay exact unless rounding is requested.

## Question 1

<p><strong>Question 1.</strong> Solve $x^2-9x+20=0$.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find both real roots of $x^2-9x+20=0$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Factorise and use the zero-product law.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Choose $-4$ and $-5$: their product is $20$ and sum is $-9$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$(x-4)(x-5)=0$.</p>
<p>$x-4=0$ or $x-5=0$.</p>
<p>$x=4$ from the first factor.</p>
<p>$x=5$ from the second factor.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $x=4$ or $x=5$ (exact integers).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> Use the zero-product law after finding a matching factor pair.</aside>

## Question 2

<p><strong>Question 2.</strong> Solve $2x^2-5x-3=0$.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find both real roots of $2x^2-5x-3=0$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Split the middle term and factor by grouping.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> The product $ac=2(-3)=-6$; $-6+1=-5$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$2x^2-6x+x-3=0$.</p>
<p>$2x(x-3)+(x-3)=0$.</p>
<p>$(2x+1)(x-3)=0$.</p>
<p>$2x+1=0$ or $x-3=0$.</p>
<p>$2x=-1$.</p>
<p>$x=-\frac12$.</p>
<p>$x=3$.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $x=-\frac12$ or $x=3$ (exact).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> For non-monic quadratics, choose split terms whose product equals $ac$.</aside>

## Question 3

<p><strong>Question 3.</strong> Complete the square for $f(x)=x^2+8x+7$ and find its minimum.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find the completed square and global minimum for real $x$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Use $x^2+2hx=(x+h)^2-h^2$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Here $2h=8$, so $h=4$ and $h^2=16$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$f(x)=x^2+8x+16-16+7$.</p>
<p>$f(x)=(x+4)^2-16+7$.</p>
<p>$f(x)=(x+4)^2-9$.</p>
<p>$(x+4)^2\ge0$.</p>
<p>$f(x)\ge-9$.</p>
<p>Equality requires $x+4=0$.</p>
<p>Thus $x=-4$.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $f(x)=(x+4)^2-9$; minimum $-9$ at $x=-4$ (exact).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> A nonnegative completed square exposes the minimum.</aside>

## Question 4

<p><strong>Question 4.</strong> Solve $2x^2-4x-1=0$ by the quadratic formula.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find both real roots in exact form.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Use $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ and $x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt\Delta}{2a}$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Set $a=2$, $b=-4$, $c=-1$; $\Delta=(-4)^2-4(2)(-1)$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$\Delta=16-4(2)(-1)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=16-8(-1)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=16-(-8)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=16+8$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=24$.</p>
<p>$x=\frac{-(-4)\pm\sqrt{24}}{2(2)}$.</p>
<p>$x=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{24}}{2(2)}$.</p>
<p>$x=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{24}}{4}$.</p>
<p>$x=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{4\cdot6}}{4}$.</p>
<p>$x=\frac{4\pm2\sqrt6}{4}$.</p>
<p>$x=\frac{2\pm\sqrt6}{2}$.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $x=\frac{2+\sqrt6}{2}$ or $x=\frac{2-\sqrt6}{2}$ (exact surds).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> Keep the sign of $b$ and divide the entire numerator by $2a$.</aside>

## Question 5

<p><strong>Question 5.</strong> How many real roots does $3x^2+2x+4=0$ have?</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Classify the real roots.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Use the sign of $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ for $a\ne0$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Set $a=3$, $b=2$, $c=4$; $\Delta=2^2-4(3)(4)$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4-4(3)(4)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4-12(4)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4-48$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=-44$.</p>
<p>$\Delta&lt;0$.</p>
<p>Therefore no real root exists.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $\Delta=-44$; no real roots (exact classification).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> A negative discriminant rules out real roots.</aside>

## Question 6

<p><strong>Question 6.</strong> Find $k$ such that $x^2-2kx+k+2=0$ has a repeated root; find that root.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find every real $k$ and its repeated root.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Set $\Delta=0$, then use $x=-b/(2a)$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Set $a=1$, $b=-2k$, $c=k+2$; $\Delta=(-2k)^2-4(1)(k+2)$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4k^2-4(1)(k+2)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4k^2-4(k+2)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4k^2-4k-8$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4(k^2-k-2)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4(k-2)(k+1)$.</p>
<p>$4(k-2)(k+1)=0$.</p>
<p>$(k-2)(k+1)=0$.</p>
<p>$k-2=0$ or $k+1=0$.</p>
<p>$k=2$ or $k=-1$.</p>
<p>$x=-(-2k)/(2\cdot1)$.</p>
<p>$x=2k/2$.</p>
<p>$x=k$.</p>
<p>For $k=2$, $x=2$.</p>
<p>For $k=-1$, $x=-1$.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $(k,x)=(2,2)$ or $(-1,-1)$ (exact).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> A repeated root occurs at zero discriminant and equals the vertex abscissa.</aside>

## Question 7

<p><strong>Question 7.</strong> Solve $x^2-2x-8\le0$.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find the complete real solution set.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Factor and test the signs on intervals between the roots.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> The numbers $-4$ and $2$ multiply to $-8$ and add to $-2$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$(x-4)(x+2)\le0$.</p>
<p>$x-4=0$ gives $x=4$.</p>
<p>$x+2=0$ gives $x=-2$.</p>
<p>For $x&lt;-2$, both factors are negative and their product is positive.</p>
<p>For $-2&lt;x&lt;4$, the first factor is negative and the second positive, so their product is negative.</p>
<p>For $x&gt;4$, both factors are positive and their product is positive.</p>
<p>At $x=-2$, the product is zero.</p>
<p>At $x=4$, the product is zero.</p>
<p>The non-strict inequality includes both zeros.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $-2\le x\le4$ (exact closed interval).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> A nonpositive upward-opening quadratic includes the interval between its roots.</aside>

## Question 8

<p><strong>Question 8.</strong> Solve $x+\sqrt x-20=0$ over the reals.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find admissible real $x$, with $x\ge0$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Put $t=\sqrt x\ge0$ and $x=t^2$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Substitute $x=t^2$: $t^2+\sqrt x-20=0$; substitute $\sqrt x=t$: $t^2+t-20=0$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$t^2+t-20=(t+5)(t-4)$.</p>
<p>$(t+5)(t-4)=0$.</p>
<p>$t+5=0$ or $t-4=0$.</p>
<p>$t=-5$ or $t=4$.</p>
<p>Reject $t=-5$ because $t\ge0$.</p>
<p>$x=4^2$.</p>
<p>$x=16$.</p>
<p>Substitute into the original left side: $16+\sqrt{16}-20$.</p>
<p>$16+4-20$.</p>
<p>$20-20$.</p>
<p>$0$.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $x=16$ (exact; original equation verified).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> Apply the nonnegative square-root restriction before recovering $x$.</aside>

## Question 9

<p><strong>Question 9.</strong> Find where $y=x+1$ meets $y=x^2-3x+5$.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find all real intersection coordinates.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Eliminate $y$ and solve the resulting quadratic.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Equate the expressions: $x+1=x^2-3x+5$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>Subtract $x$: $1=x^2-4x+5$.</p>
<p>Subtract $1$: $0=x^2-4x+4$.</p>
<p>Factorise: $0=(x-2)^2$.</p>
<p>Take the root of zero: $x-2=0$.</p>
<p>Add $2$: $x=2$.</p>
<p>Substitute in the line: $y=2+1$.</p>
<p>Add: $y=3$.</p>
<p>Check the curve: $2^2-3(2)+5$.</p>
<p>Square: $4-3(2)+5$.</p>
<p>Multiply: $4-6+5$.</p>
<p>Subtract: $-2+5$.</p>
<p>Add: $3$.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> One intersection $(2,3)$ (exact).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> A repeated root after elimination represents a tangency.</aside>

## Question 10

<p><strong>Question 10.</strong> Find the sum, product and roots of $2x^2-7x+3=0$.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find $r+s$, $rs$, and $r,s$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Use $r+s=-b/a$, $rs=c/a$, then factorise.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Set $a=2$, $b=-7$, $c=3$: $r+s=-(-7)/2$ and $rs=3/2$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$r+s=7/2$.</p>
<p>$rs=3/2$.</p>
<p>$2x^2-6x-x+3=0$.</p>
<p>$2x(x-3)-(x-3)=0$.</p>
<p>$(2x-1)(x-3)=0$.</p>
<p>$2x-1=0$ or $x-3=0$.</p>
<p>$2x=1$.</p>
<p>$x=1/2$.</p>
<p>$x=3$.</p>
<p>Check the sum: $1/2+3=7/2$.</p>
<p>Check the product: $(1/2)3=3/2$.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> Sum $\frac72$, product $\frac32$; roots $\frac12$ and $3$ (exact).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> The coefficient identities check the roots independently.</aside>

## Question 11

<p><strong>Question 11.</strong> Find the maximum of $g(x)=-x^2+4x+1$ for real $x$.</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find the maximum and where it occurs.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Complete the square and negate its nonnegativity.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Factor the leading minus: $g(x)=-(x^2-4x)+1$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>Half the inner coefficient: $-4/2=-2$.</p>
<p>Square it: $(-2)^2=4$.</p>
<p>$x^2-4x=(x-2)^2-4$.</p>
<p>$g(x)=-((x-2)^2-4)+1$.</p>
<p>$g(x)=-(x-2)^2+4+1$.</p>
<p>$g(x)=-(x-2)^2+5$.</p>
<p>$(x-2)^2\ge0$.</p>
<p>$-(x-2)^2\le0$.</p>
<p>$g(x)\le5$.</p>
<p>Equality requires $x-2=0$.</p>
<p>Thus $x=2$.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $g(x)=-(x-2)^2+5$; maximum $5$ at $x=2$ (exact).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> A negative square produces a global maximum at its vertex.</aside>

## Question 12

<p><strong>Question 12.</strong> For which real $m$ does $x^2+2mx+m+3=0$ have no real roots?</p>

<section class="solution">
<p class="solution-label">Five-step solution</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><p><strong>Step 1 — Given and required.</strong> Find the entire parameter interval.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 2 — Formula or method.</strong> Require $\Delta&lt;0$ and solve the upward-opening quadratic inequality in $m$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 3 — Substitution.</strong> Set $a=1$, $b=2m$, $c=m+3$; $\Delta=(2m)^2-4(1)(m+3)$.</p></li>
<li><p><strong>Step 4 — Complete working.</strong> One operation or inference per line.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4m^2-4(1)(m+3)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4m^2-4(m+3)$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4m^2-4m-12$.</p>
<p>$\Delta=4(m^2-m-3)$.</p>
<p>Require $m^2-m-3&lt;0$.</p>
<p>The boundary equation is $m^2-m-3=0$.</p>
<p>Its discriminant is $(-1)^2-4(1)(-3)$.</p>
<p>Square: $1-4(1)(-3)$.</p>
<p>Multiply by $1$: $1-4(-3)$.</p>
<p>Multiply: $1-(-12)$.</p>
<p>Add: $13$.</p>
<p>Use the formula: $m=\frac{-(-1)\pm\sqrt{13}}{2(1)}$.</p>
<p>Simplify numerator: $m=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{13}}{2(1)}$.</p>
<p>Simplify denominator: $m=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{13}}2$.</p>
<p>The upward-opening quadratic is negative between these boundary roots.</p>
<p>Strict inequality excludes the endpoints.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Step 5 — Final answer with precision.</strong> $\frac{1-\sqrt{13}}2&lt;m&lt;\frac{1+\sqrt{13}}2$ (exact open interval).</p></li>
</ol>
</section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><strong>Key takeaway.</strong> A positive-leading quadratic is negative only between its distinct real roots.</aside>
