# Computer Science exam overview

Cambridge International AS &amp; A Level Computer Science (9618) assesses computational thinking, programming and the theory of computer systems. This page is a study-planning guide; it is not a substitute for the syllabus for your examination year.

## Check your assessment route first

At AS Level you take Papers 1 and 2. At A Level you take all four papers, either in the same series or staged over two years with the AS Level result carried forward. Before you make a revision timetable:

1. Find the official 9618 syllabus for the year in which you will sit the examination.
2. Confirm with your teacher which route and which examination series you are entered for, and which language you will use in Paper 4 — Java, Visual Basic or Python.
3. Make a checklist of the learning outcomes: sections 1–12 for AS Level, all 20 sections for A Level.
4. Check the examination timetable and any current instructions from Cambridge International.

## What to practise

The syllabus is organised into 20 sections: theory in Papers 1 and 3, problem-solving and programming in Papers 2 and 4. Across sections, practise the following examination habits:

- **Work without a calculator.** Calculators must not be used in any 9618 paper, so practise conversions and file-size calculations by hand.
- **Write Cambridge pseudocode.** Paper 2 answers are written in pseudocode; use the keywords and structure of the pseudocode guide for your year.
- **Trace before you write.** Follow algorithms line by line in a trace table, recording each variable as it changes.
- **Use the technical term.** Theory questions reward precise vocabulary — name the register, the protocol, the normal form.
- **Program on a computer.** Paper 4 is taken on a computer in Java, Visual Basic or Python, in console mode and without internet access; practise writing, running and testing complete programs.
- **Justify for the situation.** Many questions ask you to justify a choice — a topology, a licence, a compression method, a loop structure — for the scenario given; tie the reason to that scenario.
