## Problem 1 — Base units

<p><a href="{{ '/physics/handouts/physical-quantities/' | relative_url }}#problem-1">Read problem 1</a> <span class="pts">[3]</span></p>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Identify each base quantity</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>The SI base unit for mass is the kilogram.</p></li>
<li><p>Its symbol is $\mathrm{kg}$.</p></li>
<li><p>The SI base unit for amount of substance is the mole.</p></li>
<li><p>Its symbol is $\mathrm{mol}$.</p></li>
<li><p>The SI base unit for luminous intensity is the candela.</p></li>
<li><p>Its symbol is $\mathrm{cd}$.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Final answer</strong></p><p>(a) kilogram ($\mathrm{kg}$); (b) mole ($\mathrm{mol}$); (c) candela ($\mathrm{cd}$).</p></aside>

## Problem 2 — Length prefixes

<p><a href="{{ '/physics/handouts/physical-quantities/' | relative_url }}#problem-2">Read problem 2</a> <span class="pts">[4]</span></p>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Convert using unit ratios</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{mm}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$.</p></li>
<li><p>$4.8\,\mathrm{mm}\times\frac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm m}{1\,\mathrm{mm}}=4.8\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mu\mathrm m=10^{-6}\,\mathrm m$.</p></li>
<li><p>$4.8\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm m\times\frac{1\,\mu\mathrm m}{10^{-6}\,\mathrm m}=4.8\times10^3\,\mu\mathrm m$.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Final answer</strong></p><p>(a) $4.8\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$; (b) $4.8\times10^3\,\mu\mathrm m$ (two significant figures).</p></aside>

## Problem 3 — Squared units

<p><a href="{{ '/physics/handouts/physical-quantities/' | relative_url }}#problem-3">Read problem 3</a> <span class="pts">[4]</span></p>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Square the entire length factor</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm}=10^{-2}\,\mathrm m$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^2}=(10^{-2}\,\mathrm m)^2$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^2}=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$720\,\mathrm{cm^2}\times\left(\frac{10^{-2}\,\mathrm m}{1\,\mathrm{cm}}\right)^2=720\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$720\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}=7.2\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Final answer</strong></p><p>$7.2\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ (two significant figures).</p></aside>

## Problem 4 — Cubed units

<p><a href="{{ '/physics/handouts/physical-quantities/' | relative_url }}#problem-4">Read problem 4</a> <span class="pts">[5]</span></p>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">First convert to cubic metres</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm}=10^{-2}\,\mathrm m$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=(10^{-2}\,\mathrm m)^3$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$25\,\mathrm{cm^3}\times\left(\frac{10^{-2}\,\mathrm m}{1\,\mathrm{cm}}\right)^3=25\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$25\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}=2.5\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Then convert to cubic millimetres</p><ol class="steps" start="6">
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm}=10\,\mathrm{mm}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=(10\,\mathrm{mm})^3$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=10^3\,\mathrm{mm^3}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$25\,\mathrm{cm^3}\times\left(\frac{10\,\mathrm{mm}}{1\,\mathrm{cm}}\right)^3=25\times10^3\,\mathrm{mm^3}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$25\times10^3\,\mathrm{mm^3}=2.5\times10^4\,\mathrm{mm^3}$.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Final answer</strong></p><p>(a) $2.5\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}$; (b) $2.5\times10^4\,\mathrm{mm^3}$ (two significant figures).</p></aside>

## Problem 5 — Density

<p><a href="{{ '/physics/handouts/physical-quantities/' | relative_url }}#problem-5">Read problem 5</a> <span class="pts">[5]</span></p>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Convert numerator and denominator separately</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm g=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm}=10^{-2}\,\mathrm m$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=(10^{-2}\,\mathrm m)^3$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1.25\,\frac{\mathrm g}{\mathrm{cm^3}}\times\frac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}}{1\,\mathrm g}\times\left(\frac{1\,\mathrm{cm}}{10^{-2}\,\mathrm m}\right)^3=1.25\times\frac{10^{-3}}{10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1.25\times\frac{10^{-3}}{10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}=1.25\times10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Final answer</strong></p><p>$1.25\times10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ (three significant figures).</p></aside>

## Problem 6 — Derived unit

<p><a href="{{ '/physics/handouts/physical-quantities/' | relative_url }}#problem-6">Read problem 6</a> <span class="pts">[6]</span></p>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Form units from the physical relation</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>In $E=\tfrac12 mv^2$, the numerical factor $\tfrac12$ is dimensionless.</p></li>
<li><p>The SI mass unit is $\mathrm{kg}$.</p></li>
<li><p>The SI speed unit is $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Square the speed unit: $(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})^2=\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Multiply by the mass unit: $[E]=\mathrm{kg}\times\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Combine: $[E]=\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Replace SI base units with dimensions: $[E]_{\mathrm{dim}}=ML^2T^{-2}$.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Final answer</strong></p><p>SI base units $\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}}$; dimensions $ML^2T^{-2}$.</p></aside>

## Problem 7 — Pressure

<p><a href="{{ '/physics/handouts/physical-quantities/' | relative_url }}#problem-7">Read problem 7</a> <span class="pts">[7]</span></p>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Convert force and squared area before division</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{kN}=10^3\,\mathrm N$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1.8\,\mathrm{kN}\times\frac{10^3\,\mathrm N}{1\,\mathrm{kN}}=1.8\times10^3\,\mathrm N$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm}=10^{-2}\,\mathrm m$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^2}=(10^{-2}\,\mathrm m)^2$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{cm^2}=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$24\,\mathrm{cm^2}\times\left(\frac{10^{-2}\,\mathrm m}{1\,\mathrm{cm}}\right)^2=24\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$24\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}=2.4\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Substitute in $p=F/A$: $p=\frac{1.8\times10^3\,\mathrm N}{2.4\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Divide: $p=7.5\times10^5\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Identify $\mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}=\mathrm{Pa}$: $p=7.5\times10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$1\,\mathrm{kPa}=10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$7.5\times10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}\times\frac{1\,\mathrm{kPa}}{10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}}=7.5\times10^2\,\mathrm{kPa}$.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Final answer</strong></p><p>$7.5\times10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}=7.5\times10^2\,\mathrm{kPa}$ (two significant figures).</p></aside>

## Problem 8 — Constant and dimensions

<p><a href="{{ '/physics/handouts/physical-quantities/' | relative_url }}#problem-8">Read problem 8</a> <span class="pts">[6]</span></p>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Rearrange and derive the constant's unit</p><ol class="steps">
<li><p>Rearrange $F=cv^2$: $c=F/v^2$.</p></li>
<li><p>$[F]=\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>$[v]=\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Square: $[v^2]=\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Divide: $[c]=\frac{\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}}{\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Cancel powers: $[c]=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}}$.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<section class="solution"><p class="solution-label">Calculate and check</p><ol class="steps" start="7">
<li><p>Substitute: $c=\frac{12\,\mathrm N}{(3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})^2}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Square speed: $c=\frac{12\,\mathrm N}{9.0\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Divide numbers: $c=1.333\ldots\,\mathrm{N\,s^2\,m^{-2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Replace newton with its base units: $c=1.333\ldots\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Round to two significant figures: $c=1.3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Check $[cv^2]=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}}\times\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>Combine powers: $[cv^2]=\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}$.</p></li>
<li><p>This matches the SI base unit for force.</p></li>
</ol></section>
<aside class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Final answer</strong></p><p>$[c]=\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}}$ and $c=1.3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}}$ (two significant figures).</p></aside>
